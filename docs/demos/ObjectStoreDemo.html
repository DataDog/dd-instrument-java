ObjectStore demo

Animated comparison of object-store capacity and generations in dd-instrument-java.

Eight object stores receive a steady, uneven stream of new keys. Compare independent map limits with a shared store whose shards age at their own pace.

Illustrates object-store behavior in dd-instrument-java 0.0.6, released 5 October 2026.

0.0 s

A store ID hashes to one of eight shards. Stores sharing a shard also share its generation turnover. Quiet shards age slowly; an idle shard does not age.

Each map has a 50,000-entry limit. Once full, subsequent puts are ignored until entries are removed. The quieter maps still have room.

Workload and model

Illustrative store IDs: A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4, E=5, F=6, G=8, H=9. Routing follows (storeId * 0x9E3779B9) >>> 29. Rates are 6,000 / 4,000 / 2,500 / 1,500 / 1,000 / 700 / 400 / 200 new object puts per simulated second, respectively. Both animations replay the identical 60-second workload.

All keys are unique and remain reachable; there are no removes, repeated-key updates, background cleanup, or garbage collection. One cell represents 500 entries; partial cells show partial batches. Global ageing is drawn at the idealized 16,000-young-entry boundary. The implementation samples capacity about once per 1,024 writes per thread, so real boundaries can overshoot. Ageing creates an empty young map, keeps the previous young as old, and discards the previous old. There is no time-based expiry or promotion on reads. The 32,000-entry shard ceiling is a target; background cleanup can trim old content toward 24,000.
